\section{Schema \& Data Model}
InfluxDB organises data as:
\begin{center}
Organisation $\rightarrow$ Bucket $\rightarrow$ Measurement $\rightarrow$ Tags + Fields + Timestamp
\end{center}

\subsection{Organisation}
A single InfluxDB organisation \texttt{qut-microgrid} is used, consistent with the Telegraf output configuration.

\subsection{Buckets}
\begin{table}[H]
  \caption{Buckets}
  \begin{center}
    \begin{tabular}{lll}
      \hline
      \textbf{Bucket} & \textbf{Purpose} & \textbf{Retention} \\
      \hline
      \texttt{telemetry} & JSON snapshots via Telegraf & 90 days \\
      \texttt{waveform} & Full-rate samples via \texttt{daq2-ingest} & \texttt{RAW\_RETENTION} (default 7 days) \\
      \texttt{waveform\_1s} & Per-second aggregates via \texttt{daq2-ingest} & Indefinite \\
      \hline
    \end{tabular}
  \end{center}
\end{table}

The bucket name \texttt{telemetry} matches the Telegraf output configuration. The remainder of this section describes the \texttt{telemetry} schema; the \texttt{waveform} and \texttt{waveform\_1s} schemas are described in Section~\ref{sec:fullrate}. The \texttt{telemetry\_1m} bucket of the original design was superseded by \texttt{waveform\_1s} (Section~\ref{sec:downsampling}).

\subsection{Measurement}
Burst points are written to the measurement \texttt{mqtt\_consumer}, Telegraf's default for the MQTT consumer plugin, and the per-sample points produced from them to \texttt{samples}, with the same tags and the fields \texttt{v} and \texttt{sync}. The electrical quantity is distinguished by the \texttt{subsystem} tag rather than by measurement name. This departs from the original design, which mapped each subsystem to its own measurement; a single measurement keeps the Telegraf configuration minimal, and filtering on an indexed tag is equally efficient.

\subsection{Tags}
Tags are string-valued, indexed, and low-cardinality. All tags are populated by Telegraf from the four-level MQTT topic.

\begin{table}[H]
  \caption{Tags}
  \begin{center}
    \begin{tabular}{llp{0.25\linewidth}p{0.3\linewidth}}
      \hline
      \textbf{Tag Key} & \textbf{Source} & \textbf{Example Values} & \textbf{Notes}\\
      \hline
      \texttt{node\_id} & Topic segment 2 & \texttt{node\_01}, \texttt{node\_02} & Fixed at firmware flash time; MAC/IP addresses explicitly avoided \\
      \texttt{subsystem} & Topic segment 3 & \texttt{voltage}, \texttt{current}, \texttt{ptp\_status} & Quantity; implies units (V or A) for the electrical subsystems \\
      \texttt{metric} & Topic segment 4 & \texttt{phase\_a}, \texttt{phase\_b}, \texttt{phase\_c} & Phase \\
      \texttt{topic} & Full topic & \path{microgrid/node_01/voltage/phase_a} & Added by Telegraf's \texttt{topic\_tag} \\
      \hline
    \end{tabular}
  \end{center}
\end{table}

\subsection{Fields}
Telegraf's JSON parser flattens the payload's \texttt{v} array into indexed fields, so each burst message becomes one point with 22 fields.

\begin{table}[H]
  \caption{Fields}
  \begin{center}
    \begin{tabular}{llp{0.55\linewidth}}
      \hline
      \textbf{Field Key} & \textbf{Type} & \textbf{Meaning}\\
      \hline
      \texttt{v\_0} \ldots\ \texttt{v\_19} & float & Sample values in V or A, from the payload's \texttt{v} array \\
      \texttt{dt\_ns} & float & Interval between samples in nanoseconds \\
      \texttt{sync} & float & 1 if the node's PTP servo was locked, otherwise 0 \\
      \hline
    \end{tabular}
  \end{center}
\end{table}

PTP status messages (\texttt{subsystem} = \texttt{ptp\_status}, \texttt{metric} = \texttt{servo}) are stored in the same measurement, one point per Sync, with their own fields:

\begin{table}[H]
  \caption{PTP Status Fields}
  \begin{center}
    \begin{tabular}{llp{0.55\linewidth}}
      \hline
      \textbf{Field Key} & \textbf{Type} & \textbf{Meaning}\\
      \hline
      \texttt{offset\_ns} & float & Measured clock offset from the grandmaster \\
      \texttt{path\_delay\_ns} & float & Mean path delay used in the offset calculation \\
      \texttt{freq\_ppb} & float & Frequency correction applied by the servo \\
      \texttt{state} & float & Servo state: 0 no Sync yet, 1 estimating frequency, 2 PI loop running \\
      \texttt{locked} & float & 1 once eight consecutive offsets are within 1~\textmu s \\
      \texttt{stepped} & float & 1 if this update stepped the clock \\
      \texttt{utc\_offset\_s} & float & TAI$-$UTC offset applied to \texttt{ts} \\
      \texttt{utc\_announced} & float & 1 if that offset came from the grandmaster's Announce, 0 if it is the 37~s default \\
      \hline
    \end{tabular}
  \end{center}
\end{table}

Each node's firmware version (\texttt{subsystem} = \texttt{status}, \texttt{metric} = \texttt{firmware}) is stored as one point per MQTT connection with the string field \texttt{firmware}, the output of \texttt{git describe} when the firmware was built. It is the only string field kept, through Telegraf's \texttt{json\_string\_fields}.

\subsection{Timestamp}
The point timestamp is the payload's \texttt{ts}: the time of the first sample in the burst, from the node's PTP-disciplined clock, written at nanosecond precision. The historian does not substitute broker receive time or Telegraf processing time. The time of sample $k$ is reconstructed at query time as \texttt{\_time} $+ k \cdot$ \texttt{dt\_ns}.
